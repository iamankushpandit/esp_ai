# Reference notes: conformer-stt-s3 (open-vocabulary offline STT)

Source: `.refs/conformer-stt-s3`, a clone of https://github.com/lspr98/conformer-stt-s3 at HEAD `a714e47`
("added license", 2026-07-19). All paths below are relative to that directory. `README.md:N` means README line N.

Target: Freenove FNK0104B (ESP32-S3, 16 MB flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM, 512 KB SRAM), native ESP-IDF v6.1. The STT runs
in a TRANSCRIBING phase, shares a PSRAM arena, and must be fully torn down afterwards.

---

## TL;DR

- **Model**: a distilled, int8 (power-of-two) quantized NVIDIA `stt_en_conformer_ctc_small`. 16 layers, d=176,
  4 heads, 1024 BPE tokens plus the CTC blank, greedy CTC decoding. 13.1M params. WER is 5.6 on LS-clean and 11.1 on LS-other.
- **Runtime**: fully custom C++23 plus hand-written Xtensa SIMD assembly (`tlib`/`tlib_ops`). It does **not** use esp-dl.
  The only managed dependency is `espressif/esp-dsp ^1.8.2`, for the FFT. The project was **built for IDF v6.1**, so it matches our target.
- **Model file**: `checkpoints/model.bin` is **13,828,096 B** and is committed in the repo, not tracked with LFS. It gets flashed to a raw
  14 MB `data` partition at the hard-coded address 0x110000, and the whole thing is `spi_flash_mmap`'d.
- **Weights are NOT used in place from flash** for the encoder. Just before each operator runs, its weight tensors are
  **memcpy'd from the mmap into a 256 KB internal-SRAM arena**. After the op they are freed. Only three pre-encode/pos-enc tensors
  (~1.07 MB) are read directly from the mmap.
- **Memory**: 256 KB **internal SRAM** arena plus 4 MB **PSRAM** arena, both static BSS in the reference. The documented peak at
  the 23-chunk maximum (8.28 s of audio) is **259,072 B SRAM and 3,932,416 B PSRAM** (`README.md:44`).
- **Speed**: not real-time. The RTF is about 1.2 to 1.65, and it gets worse with length because attention is O(n²). Latency after
  end-of-speech is about 1.3 s for 1 s of audio and about 13.4 s for 8.3 s of audio.
- **SD→PSRAM full copy is impossible on our board**: the 13.8 MB model plus the 4 MB arena exceeds 8 MB of PSRAM.
- **Recommendation**: keep the model in a **flash partition, memory-mapped**, as the reference does. If flash must be freed, the realistic
  fallback is **layer-by-layer streaming from SD into the SRAM arena**, with about 1.16 MB of small hot tensors kept resident.
  Details in section 6.

## 2. Model

- **Origin**: distilled from NVIDIA `stt_en_conformer_ctc_small` (13M) into a **13.1M-parameter** student (`README.md:2`).
- **Architecture** (from code and parsed `model.bin`):
  - Pre-encode subsampling (4×). Conv2d 1→176, 4×4 kernel, stride 2, ReLU. The kernel was padded from 3×3 to 4×4 so the inner dim is a multiple of 16
    (`conformer_pre_encode.cpp:21-28`, `checkpoints/README.md:10`). Then conv2d 176→176, 3×3, stride 2, ReLU, with **no bias**
    (`conformer_pre_encode.cpp:30-38`, `checkpoints/README.md:11`). Then a linear layer 3520→176 with no bias (`conformer_pre_encode.cpp:46-50`). 80 mel bins become 20
    after subsampling, and 176×20 = 3520.
  - **16 conformer layers, d_model = 176** (`conformer.h:9,11`). Each layer is macaron FF (×0.5), then rel-pos MHSA, then conv module, then FF (×0.5), then
    LayerNorm (`conformer_layer.cpp:40-99`).
  - FF: 176→704 (ReLU) →176 (`conformer_feedforward.cpp:19-31`; shapes (704,176)/(176,704) in model.bin). **SiLU was replaced by ReLU**
    (`checkpoints/README.md:6`).
  - MHSA: **4 heads, head dim 44** (`conformer_attention.cpp:32-34`). It is Transformer-XL relative-position attention with `pos_bias_u/v`
    and a skew (`conformer_attention.cpp:49-207`). Q/K/V/out/pos projections are each 176×176 int8.
  - Conv module: pointwise 176→352 (as linear), GLU, depthwise conv with **kernel 32**, BatchNorm, ReLU, pointwise 176→176
    (`conformer_convolution.cpp:29-76`). The causal conv was replaced by a normal conv (`checkpoints/README.md:9`).
  - Positional encoding table: int8 [999, 176] (`POS_ENC_Q_INT8`, used in `conformer_pos_encode.cpp:13-18`).
  - Decoder (CTC head): linear 176→**1025** (`conformer.cpp:27-32`).
- **Output units**: **BPE / SentencePiece-style subwords, vocab 1024 plus blank (id 1024)**. The vocabulary is hard-coded in
  `conformer_tokenizer.cpp:11-111`, where id 0 = `" ⁇ "` (unk) and the blank is at `conformer_tokenizer.cpp:8`. The output is lower-case English (`README.md:2`).
- **Decoding**: **greedy CTC**. It takes the argmax (`inference_task.cpp:125`), collapses repeats, and drops blanks (`conformer_tokenizer.cpp:113-129`). There is no beam
  search (there is a `TODO` at `conformer_tokenizer.cpp:115`) and **no LM**.
- **Quantization**: symmetric power-of-two int8 weights and activations with int32 biases, trained with QAT after calibration
  (`README.md:146-172`). Only linear, conv, and ReLU layers are quantized. LayerNorm, softmax, bmm, BN, and mel run in fp32 (`README.md:167-170`).
  The per-layer activation shifts are hard-coded in C++ arrays (`conformer_layer.cpp:34-37`, `conformer_attention.cpp:27-30`,
  `conformer_convolution.cpp:25-27`). **This couples the firmware to this exact model.bin.**
- **Training data**: not documented beyond "a subset of the distillation dataset" (`checkpoints/README.md:13`). No training or distillation
  scripts are published. The teacher's data is NeMo ASRSET, per the NVIDIA model card (external, not in the repo).
- **Reported WER** on the HF Open ASR Leaderboard subsets (`README.md:16-26`):

  | Subset | WER % |
  |---|---|
  | AMI | 20.2 |
  | Earnings22 | 21.4 |
  | GigaSpeech | 16.6 |
  | LibriSpeech clean | 5.6 |
  | LibriSpeech other | 11.1 |
  | SPGISpeech | 9.3 |
  | VoxPopuli | 7.0 |

## 3. Audio pipeline

- **Input**: I2S mono. It is read as 32-bit Philips, left slot, 16 kHz (`microphone.cpp:12-33`), then shifted right by `kGain=11` and clamped to
  **int16** (`microphone.cpp:62-68`, `config.h:24,26`). The model is trained on 16 kHz only (`config.h:25`).
- **Chunking**: `kChunkSize = 160*36 = 5760` samples, which is **360 ms** (`config.h:55`, `README.md:49`). There is a double buffer of 2 tensors, each
  `kChunkSize*2` int16 (the int32 raw read fits in place) (`main.cpp:33-38`, `recording_task.cpp:13-14`).
- **Features**, in `conformer_preprocessing.cpp`, with the esp-dsp FFT in `src_esp32s3/conformer_preprocessing_impl_esp32s3.cpp`:
  - `n_fft = 512`, **window 512 (32 ms)**, **hop 160 (10 ms)** (`config.h:57-61`). The window is a Hann window that is pre-scaled by 1/32767, which does the int16→float
    normalization (`conformer_preprocessing.h:56-65`).
  - The FFT is radix-4 from esp-dsp `dsps_fft4r_fc32` (`..._impl_esp32s3.cpp:14,23-25`). The power spectrum keeps the first 256 bins and drops Nyquist
    (`..._impl_esp32s3.cpp:27-29`).
  - **80 mel bins**. The filterbank is a float [80,256] tensor stored in model.bin (`MEL_FILTER_FLOAT32`) and applied as an fp32 matmul
    (`conformer_preprocessing.cpp:48-52`).
  - `log(x + 2^-24)`, then **fixed per-bin mean/std** normalization from `MEL_MEAN`/`MEL_STD` in model.bin (`conformer_preprocessing.cpp:54-78`).
    The mean/std are fixed rather than per-utterance so that chunk-wise processing is possible (`checkpoints/README.md:13`).
  - Each chunk gives `1 + floor((5760-512)/160) = 33` frames (`conformer_preprocessing.cpp:29`). Pre-encode subsamples 4× to **8 encoder
    frames per chunk**. Chunks are processed independently with no STFT overlap across chunk borders.
- **Streaming pre-encode**: while recording, every chunk runs STFT, then mel, then **PreEncode** on core 1. Recording runs on core 0 (`inference_task.cpp:45`,
  `README.md:252-261`). This must finish in under 360 ms per chunk. Multicore is disabled during this phase.
- **VAD**: simple energy gate. A chunk counts as voiced if mean(|x|) > `kActivityThreshold=1000` (`inference_task.cpp:101-113`, `config.h:38`).
  Recording starts on the first voiced chunk. It ends after `kEOSChunks=3` silent chunks (1.08 s) or at `kMaxChunks=23`. A transcription needs at least `kMinChunks=3` voiced chunks,
  and the last `kPruneChunks=2` chunks are dropped (`config.h:40-46`, `inference_task.cpp:69-98`, `README.md:46-53`). The README says it detects "any
  sound, not only voice" (`README.md:47`). **For our device, replace this with push-to-talk or a proper VAD and feed a buffer directly.**
- **Max utterance: 23 chunks = 8.28 s** (`config.h:40`). The README explains why: beyond 23 chunks, the attention `p` and `key` matrices no longer fit in the
  256 KB SRAM arena (`README.md:42`). Those tensors are explicitly placed in SRAM in `conformer_attention.cpp:145,157,193-194`. The `unfold` in
  conv2d is also in SRAM (`tlib_conv2d.cpp:94`). Two more ceilings apply: the pos-enc table allows at most 498 frames, about 62 chunks
  (`conformer_pos_encode.cpp:18`), and `kMaxChunks` is a `uint8_t`.
- The audio recorded during inference is discarded (`recording_task.cpp:30-43`).

## 4. Model storage, placement, and memory

### File format (`tlib_flash.cpp`)
- Magic `"CONFORMER1\0"`, then `u32 n_tensors`, then `u64 file_size`, then `u32 offsets[n]` (`tlib_flash.cpp:37`, parser `tlib_flash.cpp:67-101`). The metadata region is
  **64 KB** (`kFlashMetaPartitionSize`, `config.h:83`). Tensor data starts at file offset 65536 (`tlib_flash.cpp:116`).
- Each tensor has a 40 B packed header: type, dims, shape[4], stride[4], shift, numel, quantized. The data follows, 16-byte aligned (`tlib_flash.cpp:20-29,152-180`).
- Parsed from the actual file: **634 tensors**, header `file_size` = 13,818,688, and the file on disk is **13,828,096 B**. The payload is 13,712,580 B:
  int8 13,242,240 + fp32 262,784 + int32 207,556. The largest tensor is `PRE_ENCODE_OUT` [176,3520] at 619,520 B. **Each conformer layer is 772,992 B**
  (39 tensors per layer, IDs contiguous: `conformer_tensor_ids.h:653`), for a total of 12,367,872 B over 16 layers. The decoder is 184,500 B, pos_enc 175,824 B, mel filter 81,920 B,
  pre-encode conv1 278,784 B, and conv0 3,520 B.

### Placement
- A raw data partition `model, data, <no subtype>, 0x110000, 14M` (`partitions.csv:4`) is written with `parttool.py` (`Makefile:12-13`,
  `README.md:118-121`). The address is **hard-coded** as `kModelFlashAddress = 0x110000` (`config.h:85`), and `esp_partition_find` is not used.
- At boot, `tlib::flash::Initialize(0x110000, OnFlashReadRequest)` runs (`main.cpp:30`). It `spi_flash_mmap`s the 64 KB metadata permanently
  (`flash_callback.cpp:26-35`), then maps the **whole remaining ~13.75 MB** as one data mapping (`tlib_flash.cpp:115-116`,
  `flash_callback.cpp:41-57`). That takes about 211 of the 64 KB MMU pages of external virtual address space. **No munmap/deinit API exists.**

### Are weights executed directly from mmap'd flash?
**Mostly no.** `LoadTensor(id, heap)` builds a `Tensor` from the mapped view, and that **allocates in the requested arena and memcpys**
(`tlib_flash.cpp:187-191`, `tlib_tensor.h:285-291`). Every encoder weight and bias is loaded with `heap::Type::SRAM` right
before its op, then freed when its scope ends (`tlib_tensor.h:372-378`). Examples: `conformer_feedforward.cpp:23-30`,
`conformer_attention.cpp:103-104,110-111,143,154-155,190-191,205-206`, `conformer_convolution.cpp:36-73`, `conformer_layer.cpp:47-97`,
`conformer.cpp:29-32`. The reason is that the right-hand matmul operand must be in SRAM, or the matmul is about 18× slower (`README.md:198-226`).

The only tensors consumed **directly from the mmap** (`MapTensor`) are:
- `PRE_ENCODE_CONV1_WEIGHT` (278,784 B), used as the left-hand operand (`conformer_pre_encode.cpp:35`).
- `PRE_ENCODE_OUT_WEIGHT` (619,520 B), used as the left-hand operand of the transposed matmul (`conformer_pre_encode.cpp:48-49`).
- `POS_ENC` (175,824 B). A slice is memcpy'd into a PSRAM tensor (`conformer_pos_encode.cpp:13-28`).

So on each utterance, about **12.55 MB is streamed flash→SRAM with memcpy** (all layers plus the decoder). On each 360 ms chunk, about 900 KB of pre-encode
weights are read in place from flash, and 82 KB of mel filters are copied.

### SRAM vs PSRAM
- **Internal SRAM arena, 256 KB** (`config.h:71`). It is a static `DRAM_ATTR` array (`main.cpp:19`). It holds the right-hand matmul
  operands (weights), the attention `p`/`key`/`value` transposes, the quantized depthwise input, the conv2d unfold buffer, the STFT window
  buffer (`conformer_preprocessing.cpp:33`), and the mel/norm vectors.
- **PSRAM arena, 4 MB** (`config.h:73`). It is a static `EXT_RAM_BSS_ATTR` array (`main.cpp:20`). It holds the default tensor location (`tlib_heap.h:22`): activations,
  attention scores, pre-encoded chunk storage, recording buffers, and FFT buffers.
- The arena allocator is a first-fit list with 16 B alignment and at most 64 blocks (`tlib_heap.cpp:17-128`, `config.h:67-69`). Its bookkeeping (`std::list`)
  uses the system heap.
- **Documented peak** at 23 chunks: **259,072 B SRAM** and **3,932,416 B PSRAM** (`README.md:44`). `tlib::heap::MaxUsage()` /
  `Summary()` exists to measure this at runtime (`tlib_heap.h:42,77`). The README gives no numbers for shorter utterances. SRAM is effectively
  saturated at 23 chunks (259,072 of 262,144).
- Other internal RAM: 4 task stacks of 6 KB each, about 24 KB (`config.h:92-98`); I2S DMA descriptors (driver default); and the main task stack of 3.5 KB
  (`sdkconfig:1549`).
- Hardware requirements stated in the README: "256KB of SRAM and 4MB of PSRAM dedicated exclusively... 14MB of flash" (`README.md:58`).

### Teardown blockers (for our TRANSCRIBING phase)
1. Both arenas are **static BSS** (`main.cpp:19-20`), so 256 KB of internal RAM and 4 MB of PSRAM are reserved for the life of the firmware.
   They must become `heap_caps_aligned_alloc(16, …, MALLOC_CAP_INTERNAL|MALLOC_CAP_8BIT)` or a slice of our PSRAM arena.
   **Risk**: a contiguous 256 KB internal block may not be available at runtime, especially with Wi-Fi/BT/LCD. Measure
   `heap_caps_get_largest_free_block(MALLOC_CAP_INTERNAL)`. Allocate early and keep it reserved, or lower `kMaxChunks` if SRAM is short.
2. `heap::Initialize` asserts it has not been initialized yet (`tlib_heap.cpp:65`), so we need a `Deinitialize()`.
3. The flash mmaps are never unmapped (`flash_callback.cpp:26-56`), so we need `munmap` of both handles. The mapping holds about 13.8 MB of MMU virtual space.
   That matters if the LLM also mmaps large flash regions.
4. The tasks loop forever (`runner.h:49-55`) and are never deleted. The co-worker registration asserts on re-register
   (`tlib_linear_shared.cpp:52`). The event group is never deleted. The I2S channel is never deleted. The `Preprocessor` holds PSRAM tensors
   (`conformer_preprocessing_impl_esp32s3.cpp:9-16`). All of these need an explicit stop/cleanup path.

## 5. Performance

Measured by the author, where inference time = latency after capture ends. Pre-encode is already done while recording (`README.md:28-40`):

| Chunks | Audio ms | DevKitC-1 N32R16V (octal flash) ms | XIAO S3 Plus N16R8 (quad flash) ms | RTF (XIAO) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1080 | 1263 | 1485 | 1.37 |
| 6 | 2160 | 2551 | 2778 | 1.29 |
| 11 | 3960 | 5022 | 5260 | 1.33 |
| 17 | 6120 | 8756 | 9015 | 1.47 |
| 23 | 8280 | 13391 | 13659 | 1.65 |

- It scales super-linearly because attention is O(n²). The per-second cost is about 1.2 to 1.65 s of compute per second of audio.
- The XIAO S3 Plus has the same memory class as our board: 16 MB quad flash and 8 MB octal PSRAM. **Expect the XIAO numbers.**
- The XIAO vs DevKitC gap is a nearly constant **~220 to 270 ms**. That is consistent with copying about 12.5 MB of weights over quad-SPI instead of octal-SPI flash.
  This is my inference; the author does not attribute the gap.
- The matmul micro-benchmark shows that operand location matters hugely for the right-hand side (SRAM 15 ms vs flash 348 ms) but barely for the left-hand side
  (flash 18 ms, PSRAM 19 ms, SRAM 15 ms) (`README.md:212-224`). Both cores are used for linear ops during inference (`README.md:183-196`).

## 6. CRITICAL: SD card instead of flash?

Sizes: full file 13,828,096 B. Encoder layers plus decoder = 12,552,372 B, which is streamed on every utterance. The "hot, small" set is conv0 3,520, conv1 278,784,
pre-encode out 619,520, pos_enc 175,824, mel filter 81,920, and mel mean/std 640, for a total of **1,160,208 B**. This set is used on every 360 ms chunk or at inference start.

SD read time for the whole file, assuming FATFS large sequential reads over SDMMC:
- 10 MB/s: **~1.38 s**
- 20 MB/s: **~0.69 s**
- For 12.55 MB: ~1.26 s at 10 MB/s and ~0.63 s at 20 MB/s.

### (a) Copy model.bin from SD into a PSRAM buffer at phase start, then run from there
- **Not feasible on the FNK0104B.** The 13.83 MB model is larger than the 8 MB PSRAM, even before the 4 MB activation arena (`config.h:73`)
  and anything else. It only works on a board with 16 MB or more of PSRAM, like the author's DevKitC N32R16V.
- If it did fit, the code change would be trivial. `tlib::flash::Initialize()` already takes a callback that returns a pointer
  (`tlib_flash.h:33`, `tlib_flash.cpp:103-123`). The host build does exactly this with a RAM buffer (`main_64/main64.cpp:26-43,53-54`).
  You would fread the file into a 16-aligned PSRAM buffer and pass a callback that returns `buf + (addr - base)`.

### (b) Stream weights from SD layer-by-layer
This fits the existing design well, because the code already fetches **each weight tensor just in time into the SRAM arena and frees it
afterwards**. Required changes:
1. **Replace the `tlib_flash` backend.** At init, read the 64 KB metadata and the 634 × 40 B tensor headers (about 28 KB) into RAM. Reimplement
   `LoadTensor(id, heap)` as "allocate in arena, then `fseek` + `fread` the payload directly into it". The SRAM arena is internal and DMA-capable, and 16 B alignment
   satisfies SDMMC's 4 B DMA alignment. Consider repacking model.bin so each tensor's data starts on a 512 B sector boundary. That lets FATFS
   DMA straight into the destination instead of going through its sector window.
2. **Replace the 3 `MapTensor` call sites** (`conformer_pre_encode.cpp:35,48`, `conformer_pos_encode.cpp:13`). Load the ~1.16 MB hot set **once
   per phase into PSRAM** and keep it resident. Do not read ~900 KB from SD every 360 ms: at 10 to 20 MB/s that is 45 to 90 ms per chunk inside the
   360 ms real-time budget, and it would compete with SD traffic. Putting the left-hand operands in PSRAM does not slow the matmul (`README.md:214-221`).
3. **Optional prefetch.** Add a reader task and 2 × 772,992 B PSRAM staging buffers, so it reads layer *i+1* while layer *i* computes.
   Per-layer compute is about 0.1 to 0.8 s and a layer read is about 40 to 80 ms, so SD latency is almost fully hidden. `LoadTensor` then becomes a PSRAM→SRAM memcpy,
   which is about as fast as the current flash→SRAM copy. Without prefetch, a synchronous read adds about **+0.6 to 1.3 s per utterance**, which is +10 to 50% depending on
   length.
4. Remove the `flash_callback` component, the hard-coded `kModelFlashAddress`, and the `model` partition.
5. The PSRAM budget during TRANSCRIBING becomes 4 MB arena + 1.16 MB resident + (optionally) 1.55 MB staging, about **5.2 to 6.7 MB**.
6. Watch for contention on the SD bus if anything else uses the card during transcription. Also watch SDMMC reads from the core-1
   inference task while core 0 is doing co-worker matmuls. Both are fine, but give the SD reader task a priority below `CoInferenceTask`.

### Does PSRAM vs mmap'd flash change speed materially?
**No, not materially.** Weights are copied into SRAM before compute, so only the copy bandwidth differs. Octal PSRAM at 80 MHz is at best
about octal-flash speed. The whole flash-type effect between the author's two boards is about 0.22 to 0.27 s on a 1.5 to 13.7 s inference
(`README.md:31-40`). Holding weights in PSRAM instead of quad flash would save at most about 0.2 s per utterance, which is a few percent. The dominant costs are
compute and the O(n²) attention.

## 7. Host-side (PC) path, export pipeline

- A host build exists: `make compile` builds `build64/conformer-stt-s3` (`Makefile:7-10`, `CMakeLists.txt:15-23`, `README.md:128`). It uses generic
  C++ fallbacks: `tlib_ops/src_default/*`, and `conformer/src_default/conformer_preprocessing_impl_default.cpp`, which is a naive O(N²) DFT and not esp-dsp.
- **But `main_64/main64.cpp` does not transcribe.** It only loads model.bin into a RAM "flash" buffer and prints one tensor
  (`main64.cpp:45-61`). No WAV reader or end-to-end main is provided. It would be easy to write, about 40 lines: read a 16 kHz int16 WAV, then for each
  5760-sample chunk run `Preprocessor::Forward` and `PreEncode`, then `cat`, `conformer::Forward`, `argmax`, and `Decode`, mirroring `inference_task.cpp:45,115-133`.
  **This is the best way to validate bit-exact behaviour on a PC.** Note that the multicore shared-execution code is ESP-only.
- **No Python code at all**: no training, distillation, QAT, or export scripts, and no ONNX. The model.bin writer is not published. Only the file format is
  implied by `tlib_flash.cpp` and the ID order in `conformer_tensor_ids.h:6-10` ("enum values need to match the index in the model file").
  Re-exporting or re-quantizing is therefore impossible without reimplementing the pipeline. The quantization shifts are hard-coded in C++ (section 2).

## 9. Hardware assumptions to adapt

- **Board**: the sdkconfig is preconfigured for the Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 Plus (`README.md:64`). Its memory configuration (16 MB QIO flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM
  at 80 MHz, `sdkconfig:895-924,1349-1396`) matches the FNK0104B's stated memory. Still, regenerate the config for our board.
- **Partition table**: factory app is only 1 MB (`partitions.csv:3`). Our firmware (LLM runtime, display, and so on) will likely need more. A 14 MB model
  partition plus a 1 MB app uses essentially all 16 MB of flash, which means **no OTA and no room for other flash assets**.
- **Microphone**: an INMP441 I2S MEMS mic. Pins are **BCLK=GPIO2, WS=GPIO3, SD=GPIO1**, with L/R to GND (`config.h:18-22`, `README.md:87-95`). The read is 32-bit Philips
  std mode, left slot (`microphone.cpp:13-26`), and `kGain=11` is tuned for the INMP441 (`config.h:24`). If the FNK0104B uses a different mic
  (a PDM mic, or a codec such as ES8311), `Microphone` must be rewritten: use `i2s_pdm_rx` or the codec driver. The rest only needs 16 kHz int16 mono. Note that GPIO3 is a
  strapping pin (JTAG source) on the S3. GPIO33 to GPIO37 are taken by octal PSRAM. Check for conflicts with the board's SDMMC and LCD pins.
- **LED**: GPIO21, active-low, the XIAO user LED (`config.h:32`, `led_task.cpp:29-35`). Remove it or remap it.
- **Power**: the author needed about 800 nF on the mic supply because CPU load during pre-encode injected a 360 ms-periodic beep into the audio
  (`README.md:97-101`). Expect similar issues on any board where the mic shares a noisy 3.3 V rail.
- **Cores**: inference is on core 1. Co-inference, recording, and LED are on core 0. Priorities are near `configMAX_PRIORITIES` (`config.h:100-119`).
  Both cores are saturated during inference, so plan for display and UI to stall or run at low priority during TRANSCRIBING.

## Recommendation (storage placement)

1. **Preferred: keep `model.bin` in a flash partition, memory-mapped, as the reference does.** This setup is known to work on identical memory hardware (the XIAO N16R8),
   has zero load time, and costs no PSRAM for weights. Inference only costs the 256 KB SRAM and 4 MB PSRAM arenas, which can be freed at teardown. Changes:
   - Look the partition up with `esp_partition_find_first` + `esp_partition_mmap` and unmap at teardown.
   - Allocate both arenas dynamically: 256 KB internal (reserve early) and 4 MB from our PSRAM arena.
   - Add stop/deinit paths for the tasks, co-worker, I2S, and heap.

   The cost is **14 MB of the 16 MB flash**. That works only if the LLM weights and other big assets live on SD, and the app stays under about 1.9 MB.
2. **If flash space is needed elsewhere: stream from SD (option b)**, with the ~1.16 MB hot set resident in PSRAM, or kept in a small ~1.2 MB flash
   partition as a hybrid. Add a double-buffered prefetch to hide the ~0.6 to 1.3 s of SD reads. Expect about 1 to 2 days of refactoring in `tlib_flash`
   plus 3 call sites, and plenty of PC-side testing via a new host WAV→text main.
3. **Do not plan on SD→PSRAM full copy (option a)**: 13.8 MB does not fit in 8 MB of PSRAM.
